### What's fixed

#### A photographed page is cropped whole, not to the piece of it that was easiest to find

The detector answers with the largest clean paper-like rectangle it can score. On a still desk, in
even light, that is the page. Under a lamp, next to a shadow, or with the sheet running off the edge
of what the camera saw, it is a **piece** of the page — and the crop was made from the piece, so half
the photograph was thrown away.

Measured on nineteen fixture photographs, each a drawn page warped onto the canvas through a known
quad, the delivered area against the area the page actually covers:

| the page | covers | used to deliver | delivers now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a sheet filling the view | 0.998 | 0.327 | whole frame |
| the same, overhanging by 80 px | 0.998 | 0.570 | whole frame |
| a page under a light gradient | 0.726 | 0.377 | 0.727 |
| a page with a hard shadow on its lower half | 0.726 | 0.442 | 0.496 |
| a page under a gradient, sheet filling the view | 0.998 | 0.287 | 0.392 |
| a desk as bright as the paper | 0.726 | 0.228 | whole frame |

Across all nineteen rows the mean area error falls from 0.213 to 0.117, the median from 0.089 to
0.001, and the rows within a hundredth of the truth go from nine to twelve. **No row got worse.**

#### What the decision does

Three steps, in this order, on the preview the app already builds:

1. **The detector's first answer, as before.** If it covers at least 60 % of the frame, that answer
   stands and nothing else runs. Six of the nineteen rows are here, and every one of them is right to
   a thousandth.
2. **Does the paper reach the edge of the frame?** Four three-pixel bands along the frame's edges are
   compared with the frame's *own* paper level, read from its brightest 2 % and taken down by 12 grey
   levels so a highlight cannot set it alone. A mean of 70 % or more means the sheet runs past what
   the camera saw: there is no page-shaped crop to make, so the picture is delivered whole. This runs
   before anything else is tried, because a frame whose paper reaches its edges has no page-shaped
   answer to find.
3. **A second look.** The preview is flat-fielded — the local brightness under the page divided away —
   and detected again at a smaller working edge. That answer replaces the first only when it grows it
   by more than a hundredth of the frame and stays below 98 % of it: a repair, not a replacement.

The hundredth is not padding. The two passes resample differently, so on a frame where both find the
page they disagree by up to 0.0011 of it, while the repairs grow the answer by 0.054 to 0.349. Taking
a replacement inside that noise re-samples the page, and a reading taken on the new sampling can cross
its own threshold: on the upside-down page of prose the orientation tests cover, the two passes differ
by 0.0005, and adopting on that turned a page the app had set upright into one it could not decide at
all. So the repair is held to a gain that is a real gain.

#### What "whole frame" means when the sheet fills your view

Delivering the whole frame is the right answer for a sheet that runs past the edge of the camera —
there is no page-shaped crop to make — and it is the least-bad answer when the desk is as bright as
the paper: the page is no longer cut, at the cost of keeping some desk. Neither case loses a single
pixel of the page, which is the constraint the whole decision is built around. Every wrong answer we
measured was too *small*, never too large, so the decision can only ever grow an answer.

### Also in this release

- **Nothing else changes.** The same framing outline, the same filters, the same editor, the same
  settings. A frame with no page in it still comes back uncropped.
- **Cost.** The extra work is about 50 ms, and only on a frame the first pass failed to answer: a
  histogram, four thin bands, and then the flat-field and the second detection. It runs when the
  shutter is pressed, on a background thread, and never on the live preview, which draws its outline
  from a different call entirely.
- The suite is 288 unit tests in 30 classes (from 281 in 29), runnable on a plain JVM. The new class
  pins all nineteen rows above, including the ones that must *not* move, so a change to the gate, the
  rim test or the ceiling cannot quietly pass.
- The work order and the specification for a change are our own working notes and are not kept in the
  repository; what a release changed is said here and in the CHANGELOG.

### Known issues

- **The rim test reads brightness, not paper.** It works when the desk is darker than the page, which
  is the usual case, and it cannot work when the two are the same level: a sheet filling the view
  under a light gradient reads sides of 0.27 / 0.00 / 0.00 / 0.53 — a mean of 0.20 against the 0.70
  needed — so it is not seen as running past the view, and that row is cropped to 0.392 of a page that
  covers 0.998. Every failure that survives here comes from the same missing capability: a test for
  "is all of this paper" that does not depend on how it is lit.
- **A hard shadow on half a page** is improved to 0.496 against 0.726 and still not fixed; the
  flat-field reaches part of the way.
- **A very light desk** stays at 0.228, where it started: the second look makes it worse and is
  refused, and there is nothing left to repair from.
- **A page partly out of frame** is short by construction, because content outside the frame cannot
  be recovered. One side of that pair is within 0.09 of the truth and is the best available; the other
  is a genuine loss at 0.382 against 0.648.
- **A tall sheet that overhangs only above and below** reads 0.40 on the rim — two paper sides averaged
  with two dark ones — and stays at 0.540 against 0.799. Firing on any two sides would catch it and
  would also deliver half a desk on any frame with two bright edges.
- **These numbers are measured on fixture photographs.** They have not been re-measured on a real page
  under a real lamp, which is the check this decision still needs; the real page's own text margin is
  also narrower than the drawn one's, which moves the rim test.
- The page level is the median of the frame's brightest 2 %, which on a glossy page or in glare can be
  a highlight rather than paper, and would move the whole rim test.
- **The live outline is found on a subsampled frame.** The preview's grayscale image is made small for
  detection by skipping pixels rather than averaging them, so the detector sees a slightly more
  aliased picture here than it does on a photograph. On a busy background that can cost the live
  border confidence that a still shot keeps.
- **A border can run off the screen.** The preview fills the screen by cropping the frame, so when the
  page reaches the frame's sides — which is what happens when it fills your view — the part of the
  border tracing those sides is drawn outside the visible area. It marks where the page really is, but
  on a tall phone it can read as two lines rather than a frame.
- Folder support is unreachable end to end. No code assigns a document to a folder — every document is
  created with a null folder — so the folder filter never appears at all.
- Rename works on one document at a time: with more than one selected the Rename action is not offered
  at all. Bulk delete is correct.
- Rotating a page does not invalidate the stored OCR line boxes, so the invisible text layer of a
  searchable PDF exported after a rotation can be placed against pixels that have since moved.
  Reading and searching are unaffected; running the recognition again stores fresh positions.
- `sweepCaptures`, the cleanup of stale capture files, is written but not called from anywhere.
